Deposits and fees: what a landlord can charge

The legal limits on holding deposits, tenancy deposits and other charges in England.

Renting · 5 min read · Last reviewed 13 October 2026

In England, landlords and agents can only charge the payments the law allows. Anything else is a banned fee.

Key points
  • Rent.
  • A tenancy deposit, capped at 5 weeks' rent (6 weeks if the annual rent is £50,000 or more).
  • A holding deposit, capped at 1 week's rent.
  • Damages for breaking the tenancy agreement.
  • Reasonable fees to change or leave the tenancy, and to replace a lost key.
  • Council tax, utilities, communications and TV licence, if the agreement says you pay them.

Holding deposits

Only one holding deposit can be taken for a property at a time. Usually the deadline to agree the tenancy is the 15th day after the landlord agrees to the deposit, unless you both agree another date in writing. If the tenancy goes ahead, or the landlord decides not to proceed, the holding deposit must usually be returned within 7 days. A landlord can keep it only in set cases, such as false or misleading information, and must give written reasons within 7 days.

Your tenancy deposit must be protected

  1. The landlord or agent must protect your deposit in a government-approved scheme within 30 days of receiving it.
  2. They must give you the prescribed information about where it is held.
  3. You can ask the scheme to confirm your deposit is protected.
  4. If it was not protected properly, you may be able to claim compensation through the County Court.

Banned fees and limits

Key points
  • Reference check fees and other charges for granting a tenancy are banned.
  • A late-rent fee is only allowed once rent is at least 14 days overdue, and is capped at 3% above the Bank of England base rate on the unpaid amount.
  • Fees for changing the tenancy are capped at £50 unless the landlord can justify higher reasonable costs.
  • Councils can fine landlords up to £5,000 for a first breach, and up to £30,000 for a repeat breach within 5 years.
Tip

Ask for fees to be listed in writing before you pay anything. Pay by a method you can prove, such as bank transfer, and keep receipts.

Watch out

General information for England, not legal advice. Check the limits with Shelter or your council before paying a disputed charge.

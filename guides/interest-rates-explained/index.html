Interest rates explained

AER, gross, monthly vs annual interest, and inflation's effect on your savings.

Saving · 5 min read · Last reviewed 11 October 2026

Interest rates look simple, but the way they're quoted changes what you actually earn. Here's how to compare like for like.

The terms

  • AER (Annual Equivalent Rate): what you'd earn over a year including interest on interest. Use this to compare accounts.
  • Gross: the rate before tax.
  • Fixed vs variable: a fixed rate is locked for a term, and a variable rate can change at any time.
  • Easy access, notice and fixed term: how quickly you can get your money out.
Key points
  • £10,000 at 4% AER earns about £400 in a year
  • The same £10,000 at 1% earns about £100
  • Moving savings can be the easiest money you'll ever make

Watch the small print

  • Teaser or bonus rates that drop after a few months.
  • Tiered rates where the headline rate only applies to the first slice of your money.
  • Withdrawal limits on “easy access” accounts.
  • Interest paid monthly vs yearly. Monthly grows very slightly faster if you leave it in.

Inflation

If prices rise faster than your interest rate, your savings lose buying power even as the balance grows. That's why the rate matters so much, and why long-term money may belong in investments rather than cash.

Tip

Set a reminder for the day any bonus rate ends, then move your money if the rate drops.

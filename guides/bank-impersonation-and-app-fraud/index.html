Bank impersonation & APP fraud

Safe-account scams, 159, and reimbursement rules.

Scam Defence · 6 min read · Last reviewed 11 October 2026

In Authorised Push Payment (APP) fraud, you're tricked into sending money yourself, often by someone pretending to be your bank, the police, a seller or a loved one.

Things your bank will never do

  • Ask you to move money to a “safe account”.
  • Ask for your full PIN, password or a one-time code.
  • Ask you to install remote-access apps.
  • Ask you to keep a call secret, even from branch staff.
  • Pressure you to act immediately.

If someone calls claiming to be your bank

  1. Hang up. Caller ID can be faked (“spoofing”).
  2. Call 159 (it connects you to many UK banks), or the number on the back of your card.
  3. Use a different phone if you can, or wait 5 minutes, because some scammers keep the line open.

If you've already paid

  1. Call your bank immediately. Speed matters.
  2. Report it to Report Fraud, the national service (it replaced Action Fraud in December 2025).
  3. Keep all messages, numbers and receipts.
Key points
  • Banks must reimburse most APP fraud victims up to £85,000 per claim
  • Claims within 13 months of the last payment
  • An excess of up to £100 may apply (not for vulnerable customers)
  • Covers UK Faster Payments and CHAPS made from 7 October 2024
Watch out

You could lose protection if you ignore clear warnings or are found to be grossly negligent, so take bank warnings seriously.

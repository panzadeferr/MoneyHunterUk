Compound growth

The maths of growth, the Rule of 72 and regular investing.

Investing · 5 min read · Last reviewed 11 October 2026

Compounding means your growth earns growth. Over long periods it's the most powerful force in personal finance, for savings and for debt.

Key points
  • £2,000 growing 7% a year becomes about £3,930 in 10 years
  • £100 a month at 5% for 30 years grows to about £83,000 (you pay in £36,000)
  • Rule of 72: years to double ≈ 72 ÷ growth rate

Start early

Two people invest £100 a month at 6% until 65. The one who starts at 25 ends up with far more than the one who starts at 35, much more than the extra £12,000 paid in, because of 10 extra years of compounding.

Falls hurt more than you think

If an investment falls 50%, it needs to rise 100% to get back to where it was. A 10% rise followed by a 10% fall leaves you down 1%. That's why diversification and staying invested matter.

Regular investing

Investing a fixed amount every month (pound-cost averaging) means you automatically buy more when prices are low and less when they're high, without trying to predict the market.

Tip

Compounding works against you on debt too: a credit card balance at 24% roughly doubles in 3 years if you don't pay it.

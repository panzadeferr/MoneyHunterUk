How stacking works

The layers you can combine on one purchase, and which ones usually clash.

Cashback · 5 min read · Last reviewed 15 October 2026

Stacking means getting more than one saving on the same purchase. Each saving is a separate layer, and the skill is knowing which layers work together.

The layers

  • A sale price or a voucher code from the shop.
  • Cashback from a cashback site, paid on top of what you pay.
  • A discounted gift card or a gift card app that pays cashback.
  • A rewards or cashback credit card.
  • The shop's own loyalty points.

What usually works

  • Loyalty points plus cashback, if the cashback site allows it for that shop.
  • A cashback credit card on top of cashback from a site, because the card is only the payment method.
  • A gift card bought at a discount, then spent at the shop.

What usually clashes

  • A voucher code the cashback site doesn't list: many sites decline cashback when you use one.
  • Gift cards as payment: many shops are excluded from cashback when you pay with one, so read the shop's terms on the cashback site.
  • Browser extensions that compete: use only one at a time.
Key points
  • Every layer has its own terms. Check the shop's terms on the cashback site before you pay.
  • Order matters: see the next guide.
Watch out

Stacking is not worth it if it makes you buy something you wouldn't have bought. 10% back on a purchase you didn't need is still 90% spent.

Budgeting basics

50/30/20, needs vs wants, pay yourself first and zero-based budgets.

Budgeting · 6 min read · Last reviewed 11 October 2026

A budget is just a plan for your money before it arrives, so it goes where you want instead of disappearing. It doesn't need to be complicated.

Start with take-home pay

Budget with what actually lands in your account after tax, National Insurance, pension and student loan deductions, not your headline salary.

Three simple methods

  • 50/30/20: roughly 50% needs (rent, bills, food, transport), 30% wants, 20% savings or extra debt payments. On £2,000 take-home, that's £1,000 / £600 / £400.
  • Pots or envelopes: split money into separate pots for bills, food, fun and savings. Most banking apps support this.
  • Zero-based: give every pound a job until nothing is unassigned, including savings.
Tip

If your needs are well over 50% (common with London rents), don't panic. Use the split as a direction, not a rule.

Pay yourself first

Set an automatic transfer to savings on payday. Saving whatever's left at the end of the month rarely works.

If you have debts

  • Avalanche: pay minimums on everything, then put extra money on the highest interest rate first. This saves the most money.
  • Snowball: put extra on the smallest balance first. Quick wins keep you motivated.
  • Either beats doing nothing. Pick the one you'll stick to.
Watch out

If you can't cover essentials or minimum payments, get free help now from StepChange, Citizens Advice or MoneyHelper. Never pay for debt advice.

Small leaks add up

£5 a day is about £1,825 a year. Track your spending for one month. Most people are surprised where the money goes.

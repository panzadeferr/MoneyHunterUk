Your credit score

Hard vs soft searches, utilisation, the electoral roll and how long things stay on file.

Credit & Borrowing · 6 min read · Last reviewed 11 October 2026

Your credit file is your borrowing history, held by credit reference agencies (Experian, Equifax and TransUnion). Lenders use it to decide whether to lend to you and at what rate. Each agency's “score” is its own estimate. Lenders make their own decisions.

Check it for free

You have a legal right to see your credit file for free, and all three agencies offer free access through their own or partner services. Checking your own file is a soft search and never affects your score.

What helps

  • Being on the electoral roll at your current address.
  • Paying everything on time. Set up at least minimum-payment Direct Debits.
  • Keeping credit card balances low compared with your limits (low utilisation).
  • A long, steady history: older accounts managed well.
  • Using eligibility checkers before applying (they use soft searches).

What hurts

  • Missed or late payments, defaults and CCJs. These usually stay on your file for six years.
  • Lots of applications in a short time (hard searches are visible to lenders, usually for about a year).
  • Maxed-out cards.
  • Financial links to someone with poor credit through joint accounts or loans.
Tip

Spot something wrong on your file? Ask the agency to correct it. Mistakes are more common than you'd think.

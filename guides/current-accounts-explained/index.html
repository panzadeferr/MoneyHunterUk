Current accounts explained

Sort codes, account numbers, debit cards, Confirmation of Payee and how to pick an everyday account.

Banking & Switching · 4 min read · Last reviewed 11 October 2026

Your current account is where your pay lands and your bills leave from. Pick one that's free, has a good app and doesn't punish you for the occasional slip.

The numbers that matter

  • Sort code (6 digits): identifies your bank and branch.
  • Account number (8 digits): identifies your account.
  • Debit card: spends your own money. It's not credit, so it doesn't build a credit history the same way.

Confirmation of Payee

When you pay someone new, your bank checks that the name you typed matches the account. If it says “no match” or “close match”, stop and check with the person on a number you already have. It's one of the best scam blockers there is.

What to look for in an everyday account

  • No monthly fee (or one you get value from).
  • A clear app with spending pots, notifications and card freeze.
  • Fair overdraft pricing, if you ever need one.
  • Fee-free spending abroad if you travel.
  • FSCS protection: check the bank is UK-authorised.
Tip

Many hunters keep two accounts: a main one for bills and pay, and a spare one used only for switching. Your real finances never get disrupted.

Why banks check your ID

Anti-money-laundering rules require banks to verify who you are. Have your passport or driving licence ready. Some applications also run a credit check (see “Your credit score”).

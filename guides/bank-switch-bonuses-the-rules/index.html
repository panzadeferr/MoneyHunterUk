Bank switch bonuses: the rules

Pay-in amounts, active Direct Debits, eligibility windows, clawbacks and how to never miss a bonus.

Banking & Switching · 6 min read · Last reviewed 11 October 2026

Banks sometimes pay you a cash bonus to switch your current account to them. It's legitimate: they're competing for customers. But every offer has rules, and missing one means no bonus.

The usual conditions

  • A full switch using CASS, often within a deadline (e.g. 30 or 60 days).
  • Pay in a minimum amount, often from an external account, within a time limit.
  • Have a set number of active Direct Debits. “Active” usually means at least one payment has actually gone out.
  • Not have held an account with that bank (or bank group), or claimed a bonus from them, within a set period.
  • Sometimes: log into the app, use the debit card, or keep the account open for a period.

The 5-minute checklist before you apply

  1. Read the full terms on the bank's own site, not a summary.
  2. Check eligibility: the exclusion window, and whether sister brands count as the same bank.
  3. Write down every condition and its deadline.
  4. Set Direct Debits up early, so they've collected before the deadline.
  5. Set a calendar reminder a week before each deadline.

Clawbacks

Some offers can take the bonus back if you close the account within a set time. Look for words like “reclaim” or “withdraw the payment” in the small print.

Credit files

Many current account applications involve a hard credit search, especially with an overdraft. A few spaced-out switches are fine for most people, but avoid stacking applications before a mortgage or big loan.

Watch out

Offers change fast. Money Hunters shows live offers with the date each was last checked. Always confirm on the provider's own page before applying.

Your tax-free allowances

Personal Allowance, savings and dividend allowances, and the tax year.

Tax · 5 min read · Last reviewed 11 October 2026

Everyone has tax-free allowances. Knowing them helps you keep more of your money, and choose between savings, ISAs and investments.

Key points
  • Personal Allowance: £12,570 of income tax-free (reduced by £1 for every £2 earned over £100,000)
  • Personal Savings Allowance: £1,000 of interest tax-free for basic-rate taxpayers, £500 for higher-rate, £0 for additional-rate
  • Dividend allowance: £500
  • Capital Gains Tax annual exempt amount: £3,000
  • ISAs: everything inside is tax-free, on top of these allowances

The tax year

The UK tax year runs from 6 April to 5 April. Allowances reset each year, and most can't be carried forward.

Your tax code

Your tax code tells your employer how much tax-free pay you get. A new job can mean an emergency code and too much tax for a while. Check your personal tax account on gov.uk, and HMRC will refund any overpayment once it's corrected.

Tip

Keep your P60 (yearly pay and tax summary). You'll need it for mortgages, refunds and claims.

Watch out

Tax rates and allowances change in Budgets. Figures are for 2026/27, checked October 2026.

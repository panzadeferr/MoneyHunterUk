When stacking isn't worth it

The traps: tiny payouts, time cost, and spending to save.

Cashback · 4 min read · Last reviewed 15 October 2026

Stacking is a way to make planned spending cheaper. It stops being worth it in a few common situations.

Watch for

  • Buying something you didn't need, because there was a deal.
  • Spending an hour to save a pound or two.
  • Minimum payout limits that leave small balances stuck.
  • Cashback that never gets confirmed, because you counted it before it was paid.
  • Using credit you can't clear in full, which costs more than you earn.
  • Applying for lots of products, which can leave marks on your credit file.

A quick test

Key points
  • Would I buy this anyway? If not, stop.
  • How long will it take, and what is my time worth?
  • Can I pay it off in full this month?
Tip

Count cashback as a bonus only when it has landed in your account. The Progress tracker can show you what is pending and what is paid.

Watch out

Anyone who tells you that you can get rich from cashback is selling something. For most people it's a modest saving on planned purchases.

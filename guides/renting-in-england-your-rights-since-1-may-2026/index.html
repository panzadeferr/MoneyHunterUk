Renting in England: your rights since 1 May 2026

What changed under the Renters' Rights Act, and what it means for you as a tenant.

Renting · 5 min read · Last reviewed 13 October 2026

Most changes from the Renters' Rights Act 2025 started on 1 May 2026. This guide covers England only. Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland have their own rules.

Key points
  • Landlords can no longer end a tenancy with a 'no fault' section 21 notice.
  • New tenancies are rolling (periodic) with no fixed end date. Existing assured shorthold tenancies became periodic tenancies on 1 May 2026.
  • You can leave by giving 2 months' written notice, on the rent day or the day before.
  • A landlord who wants you out needs a legal ground and usually gives 4 months' notice, though some grounds allow less.

Rent increases

Rent can go up once a year, and not in the first 12 months. The landlord must use the official form (Form 4A) and give at least 2 months' notice. You can challenge an increase that is above market rent.

Finding a place

Key points
  • Adverts and offers must state a specific price. Landlords cannot accept or encourage offers above the advertised rent.
  • Landlords cannot ask for or accept rent before the tenancy agreement is signed. After signing, they can ask for at most 1 month's rent in advance.
  • You can ask to keep a pet. The landlord must consider it and give a reason if they refuse.
  • Landlords cannot refuse to rent to you because you receive benefits or have children.

If something goes wrong

Councils have new powers to act against landlords who break the rules. For some offences, such as renting out a property that should be licensed, tenants can apply to the First-tier Tribunal for a Rent Repayment Order, which can require repayment of up to 2 years' rent.

Tip

Keep your tenancy agreement, rent receipts and messages with your landlord. They are your evidence if there is a dispute.

Watch out

This is general information, not legal advice. If you are facing eviction or a dispute, contact Shelter, Citizens Advice or your council. Rules and dates can change, so check the gov.uk pages below.

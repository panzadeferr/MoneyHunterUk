Direct Debits & standing orders

What they are, the Direct Debit Guarantee, and cheap ways to meet switch requirements.

Banking & Switching · 4 min read · Last reviewed 11 October 2026

A Direct Debit lets a company collect money from your account on agreed dates. The amount can change, but they must tell you in advance.

Direct Debit vs standing order

  • Direct Debit: the company pulls the money. It can vary. Covered by the Direct Debit Guarantee.
  • Standing order: you push a fixed amount on set dates. You control it.
  • Card payment: neither of the above, so it doesn't count towards switch bonus rules.

The Direct Debit Guarantee

If an error is made in the amount or date of a Direct Debit, your bank must refund you straight away. You can cancel a Direct Debit at any time through your bank.

Meeting switch bonus rules

Most switch offers need a number of active Direct Debits. Usually that means each one has collected at least one payment. Everyday bills (phone, energy, council tax, gym) count. Set them up on the new account early.

Tip

Before switching, check your bank app for Direct Debits you don't recognise or don't need. A switch is a good moment for a clear-out.

Funds, platforms & fees

Index funds, platform and fund fees, and why small percentages matter.

Investing · 5 min read · Last reviewed 11 October 2026

Fees look tiny as percentages, but they're taken every year and compound against you.

The fees you'll see

  • Fund fee (ongoing charges figure, OCF): the yearly cost of running the fund.
  • Platform fee: what the app or broker charges to hold your investments.
  • Dealing fees: per buy or sell, on some platforms.
  • FX fees: when buying assets priced in other currencies.
Key points
  • 1.5% vs 0.2% on £10,000 is £130 a year
  • 0.45% a year on £20,000 is £90
  • Over decades, high fees can eat a large share of your growth

Index funds and ETFs

Index funds and ETFs track a market (like a global stock index) instead of paying managers to pick winners. That usually means lower fees and broad diversification, which is why many beginners start there.

Accumulation vs income

Accumulation units reinvest dividends automatically. Income units pay them out. For long-term growth, accumulation keeps your money compounding.

Tip

Compare platforms by total yearly cost for YOUR pot size. Flat fees suit bigger pots, percentage fees suit smaller ones.

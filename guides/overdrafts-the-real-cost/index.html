Overdrafts: the real cost

Arranged vs unarranged, interest rates, and how to get out of your overdraft.

Banking & Switching · 5 min read · Last reviewed 11 October 2026

An overdraft lets you spend more than you have. Since April 2020, FCA rules mean overdrafts are priced with a single annual interest rate. Banks can't charge fixed daily or monthly overdraft fees, and unarranged overdrafts can't cost more than arranged ones.

Key points
  • Around 40% a year is common for arranged overdrafts
  • £500 overdrawn all year at about 40% costs roughly £200
  • Banks must show an APR so you can compare

Why overdrafts are expensive borrowing

An overdraft feels like part of your balance, but at typical rates it costs far more than most personal loans or a 0% card cleared in time.

Getting out of your overdraft

  1. Find your real balance: your balance minus the overdraft.
  2. Turn off or lower the limit gradually so you can't slide back.
  3. Cut one or two non-essentials and put that money towards the overdraft each payday.
  4. If it's large, compare a cheaper way to borrow, but only if you won't run the overdraft up again.
  5. If you're struggling, talk to your bank. It must help customers in financial difficulty.
Watch out

If borrowing feels out of control, get free help from StepChange, Citizens Advice or MoneyHelper. Never pay a company for debt advice.

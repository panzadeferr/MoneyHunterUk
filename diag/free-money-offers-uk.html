How sign-up bonuses actually work

Most follow the same broad pattern regardless of category. Understanding the mechanics helps you spot the ones worth doing and the ones with too much friction for too little reward.

The offer link matters. Referral bonuses generally require the correct link or referral code — signing up direct (without the code) means no bonus. Cashback sites work the same way: the tracking cookie is set when you click through from the cashback site.

New-customer clauses are strict. Almost every offer restricts to genuinely new customers — often new to the whole company, not just new to a specific product. Some cashback sites go further: no bonus if you’ve had another product with the merchant in the past 12–24 months.

Qualifying actions vary widely. Some offers pay just for signing up (rare). Most require a qualifying action: a minimum spend, a completed purchase, or a period of activity.

Payment timing is often slow. Cashback typically shows as “tracked” within 24–72 hours and “confirmed” after 30–90 days. Sign-up bonuses have their own timelines — a week to several months.

Hold periods can void the bonus. Some require you to keep the account active for a set period; cancel or downgrade early and the bonus is withdrawn.

Stacking offers: when it works, when it doesn’t

Multiple welcome bonuses at once is legitimate provided you’ll use the services. Sensible patterns:

  • Different sites — TopCashback for browser-based checkouts + Airtime for card-linked in-store spend + JamDoughnut for gift-card purchases are independent
  • Household members — many offers allow one bonus per person, so partners signing up separately is fine (each referring the other)

Where stacking doesn’t work:

  • Same company, multiple accounts — explicitly banned by nearly every provider
  • Cashback on top of referral — some offers exclude concurrent use of cashback sites; check the offer terms

FAQ

Are sign-up bonuses taxable? Most cashback and sign-up rewards from UK companies are treated as a discount rather than income for HMRC purposes and don’t need to be declared. Larger or repeated referral income can be different — worth checking HMRC’s guidance on miscellaneous income if you’re doing this at scale.

Do I need to keep the account open after claiming? Depends on the offer. Some pay and let you close immediately with no clawback. Others have hold periods of 30 days to 12 months.

What if my bonus doesn’t track? Contact the provider (direct sign-ups) or cashback site support. Most have dispute processes. Screenshots at the point of sign-up help — take one before you close the tab.
